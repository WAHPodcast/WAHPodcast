Classroom Resources

Suggested audience & Common Core standards

Suggested audience: high school students (grades 9-12), adaptable for introductory college courses

Relevant Common Core standards:


Exploring the History

  • • Listen: Listen to the podcast episode, paying attention to hte central events, individuals, and themes.
  • • Observe: Examine the accompanying images. What do they reveal about the people, places, and events explored in the episode?
  • • Discuss: Explore the central themes, historical events, and perspectives presented in the episode. What questions or ideas emerge from the discussion?
  • • Share: Share observations and interpretations. Consider different perspectives and draw on examples from the episode to support your ideas.
  • • Reflect: How has this episode deepened or challenged your understanding of African history? What questions remain unanswered?

    • Season One: Episode One: The Women's War

    Guiding Questions: To what extent do women's protests against British imperial injustice reshape our understanding of their role in the making of colonial laws and political discourse? In what ways do such resistances challenge enduring narratives of female passivity in society?

    Key concepts

  • • Imperialism: The extension of one country's political, economic, or cultural power over another.
  • • War: A period during which a state or group is engaged in open armed conflict against another state or group.
  • • Protest: The expression of strong disapproval or opposition to something, including a law or policy.
  • • Tax: The imposition of money by an authority on a person or property to fund public services.
  • • Solidarity: An agreement between and support for members of a group to pursue a common interest.
  • • Authority: A legitiamte power that influences thorugh, opinion, and behavior.
  • • System: A means of working or doing things based on a given plan or set of rules.

    Key concepts

  • • Kingdom: A territory that is organized politically by a monarchical government with a king or queen as the head.
  • • Golden Stool: Also knows as "Sika Dwa Kofi" in Akan Twi, is a sacred symbol of the Asante people that represents their spiritual unity, authority, and identity.
  • • Military Strength: The capability in terms of personnel and material that influences the capacity to fight a war.
  • • Diplomacy: The ability to manage relationships without having recourse to force or war.
  • • Kingship: The personality, position, office, dignity of a king, or government by a king.
  • • Rebellion: An organized defiance of or opposition to an established system of authority.

    • Season One: Episode Three: The Punitive Expedition: How Benin Was Stripped of Its Wealth

    Guiding Questions: How do symbols, culture, and power intertwine, and waht happens when a society loses control over the treasures through which it preserves its history and identity?

    Key concepts

  • • Imperialism: The extension of one country's political, economic, or cultural power over another.
  • • Sovereignty: The authority of a state or society to govern itself without external interference.
  • • Autonomy: The ability to make decisions and act independently.
  • • Commodity: A product or resource that can be bought and sold.
  • • Punitive Expedition: A military campaign undertaken to punish a population or political authority for perceived resistance or wrongdoing.
  • • Restitution: The return of property or cultural objects to their rightful owners or communities.
