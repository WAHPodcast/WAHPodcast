The Punitive Expedition

How Benin Was Stripped of Its Wealth

A short history

In the late nineteenth century, the Kingdom of Benin was an autonomous and powerful West African Kingdom. For centuries, it continued to reproduce its cultural and political way of life. It had unique and sophisticated infrastructure, an established trade network, and it was renowned for its wealth and its rich cultural symbols.

Elsewhere, in Western Europe, a handful of pwerful states were competng to advance thseir power across the globe. Following the renowned Berlin Conference (1884-1885), they raced to colonize the African ocntinenet by whatever means necessary. In addition to their use of industrail military technology, European powers systematically exploited African groups. Freqently, they interfered in systems of politics and trade to create pretexts for larger-scale takeovers or land seizures. They also utilized print media propaganda and social-Darwinist rhetoric to justify their actions.

This episode tells the story of the Punitive Expedition, Britain's invasion of the Kingdom of Benin (in what is now southern Nigeria) in 1897. Although this story is unique, it nevertheless exhibits themes which are familiar across the broader African context at this point in time.

This episode explores how Benin's autonomy, stable political order, and wealth made it a target of British imperialism. It unpacks how Acting Consul James Phillips disregarded warnings not to interfere with the sacred festival of Igue, sparking a reaction from Benin. This prompted British propagandists to seize on the diplomatic incident as a pretext to invade.

Following Benin’s defeat, British officers began looting the city. Looting was undertaken to cover the expenditure of the invasion, or for personal gain. Over time, the treasures they stole became a source of capital for Western powers. Benin’s sacred kingship treasures were accordingly reduced to commodities devoid of their socio-cultural and religious context and meanings.

This episode grapples with what happens when an empire doesn’t just conquer land and people but seizes the very treasures through which a civilization remembers and governs itself. It also speaks to British imperial strategy, whereby persistent colonial intrusion (both in terms of territorial and economic sovereignty) elicited resistance that could then be used to justify fully-fledged invasion and wealth extraction.
